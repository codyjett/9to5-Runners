Nike Alphafly 4 Review

Three perspectives. 106 combined miles.

Back to Shoes

At 9 to 5 Runners, we're everyday runners balancing careers, families, and everything else life throws our way. We're not elite athletes chasing Olympic qualifying times, but that doesn't mean we don't love chasing PRs, testing the latest running shoes, and seeing just how much speed we can squeeze out of ourselves on race day.

The Nike Alphafly has become one of the most recognizable race day shoes on the market, and with the release of the Alphafly 4, we were excited to see what Nike had changed. With a reputation for delivering speed, efficiency, and a unique ride, the Alphafly has some pretty big expectations to live up to.

But here's the thing: not every runner experiences a shoe the same way. We all have different running styles, preferences, and expectations when it comes to what makes a great race day shoe.

That's why we're bringing three different perspectives to this review. We've each spent time putting the Alphafly 4 through its paces, looking at everything from fit and comfort to performance and whether the updates actually make a difference.

So, does the Alphafly 4 live up to the hype? Is it worth the premium price tag? And, more importantly, is it a shoe that makes sense for everyday runners like us?

Let's get into it.

The Good

Cody

The Alphafly 4 continues to deliver the ride I've come to love from this lineup. The combination of the carbon plate, Air Zoom units, and midsole geometry works incredibly well with my stride, helping me find a rhythm and maintain it. I'm a pretty average runner, and I'll take all the help I can get when chasing a PR. The biggest improvement for me is the updated upper. The fit feels more comfortable, the lockdown is excellent, and I genuinely think it's the biggest reason to consider upgrading from the 3. And let's not forget the looks. Nike nailed the design, and this shoe looks every bit as fast as it feels.

Blake

The Alphafly 4 is the most comfortable and stable version yet. The updated Atomknit 4 upper is more breathable in the toebox while using a tighter knit through the midfoot for a more secure lockdown. The wider fit also gives you more volume through the arch, making it more accommodating than previous versions. If you've had issues with arch blisters in earlier Alphaflys, I think you'll appreciate this update.

The midsole is noticeably softer and bouncier than the Alphafly 3. The new ZoomX LT top layer, wider plate, and updated Air Zoom units all work together to deliver that familiar Alphafly energy return, just dialed up a notch. Despite the softer ride, the wider platform makes it significantly more stable, especially when cornering. This is easily the most confident I've felt taking turns in an Alphafly without worrying about rolling an ankle.

The outsole coverage is similar to the Alphafly 3, but durability seems just as good, if not slightly better. Nike has also made it a little quieter, with less of that clunky sound from previous versions. It's still not a particularly quiet shoe, but it's an improvement.

Eric

Style - The Alphafly line has always been eye catching and that remains true with the 4th edition. Everything about the shoe looks and feels futuristic

Breathability - Atomknit remains one of the more breathable and comfortable uppers in racing shoes. It offers a unique experience over traditional uppers.

Responsiveness - The new Zoom X LT is softer and more responsive, providing some excellent propulsion from the forefoot.

Stability - I mostly found the previous versions of the shoe to be relatively stable, however a wider base makes the shoe a little more approachable for more individuals

The Bad

Cody

My biggest complaint is that I wish Nike had made the Alphafly 4 even lighter. It's certainly not a heavy shoe, and Nike did reduce the weight compared to the previous version, but four iterations in, I was hoping for a little more. My other concern is the upper's volume. Personally, I enjoy a roomier fit, but I could see runners with narrower feet struggling to get the same lockdown and secure feeling that I experienced.

Blake

The roomier upper required some extra lace adjustments to get a secure lockdown with my narrower foot. Once I dialed it in, it felt secure, but runners with narrow feet may need to spend some time getting the fit right.

While the midsole feels smooth once you're running with a midfoot or forefoot strike, I found it a little clunky when jogging between intervals. It's not a major issue, but it's noticeable when you're not running at race pace.

I also wish Nike had lowered the drop from 8mm to 5 or 6mm. For a racing shoe, I'd like a little more foam under the forefoot to potentially get even more energy return.

Finally, while the Alphafly 4 is softer, bouncier, and more stable, it doesn't feel like a massive overhaul from the Alphafly 3. It feels more refined than revolutionary, especially if you were hoping for a major leap forward.

Eric

The lockdown - While lockdown isn’t necessarily lacking, the knit tongue makes you choose between great lockdown and lace pressure. I think you can find a balance but it takes some effort

The squeak - the “clomp” of the forefoot pods has for sure been dampened…but unfortunately the new foam/pod combo makes for a very squeaky step which can be heard for miles

Underfoot feel - While the foam/pod combo makes for a responsive ride, it can also be a little bit harsh to those who prefer a more uniform or smooth roll. After 20 miles in the shoes I found that my feet tired of the forefoot compression.

The weight - As someone who prefers a lighter racer, the extra weight of the shoe when compared to other options on the market gives me pause.

The Verdict

Cody

The Nike Alphafly 4 is the most comfortable and stable Alphafly I've run in yet. The ride is fantastic, the updated upper is a meaningful improvement, and it continues to be one of my favorite race day options. I had every intention of wearing this shoe for the Philadelphia Marathon before an injury forced me to withdraw. Honestly, that's the biggest compliment I can give it. When it came time to choose a shoe I trusted for 26.2 miles, the Alphafly 4 was my pick.

Blake

The Nike Alphafly 4 is the most comfortable and stable Alphafly yet. The upper is more breathable and accommodating, the midsole is softer and bouncier, and the wider platform makes cornering much more confidence-inspiring. The outsole is also slightly quieter, and the shoe is a touch lighter.

It's a really good update, but not the major overhaul some runners were hoping for. Nike seems to have saved some of those more innovative features for the Apex. The Alphafly still delivers that unique, energetic ride that makes it such a compelling racing shoe, but this version feels more refined and accessible than outright revolutionary. If you loved the Alphafly 3, you'll probably enjoy the improvements, but I don’t think you necessarily need to rush to upgrade.

Eric

The last version of the Alphafly was incredibly popular, despite there being lighter and newer options on the market. That being said, both the 3 and 4 are shoes that just don’t quite work for my stride. I believe they are going to heavily favor forefoot strikers and those who prefer a “punchy” sensation.

Despite my personal preferences, the technology is there and I can see why people will continue to reach for this model

Worth the Price?

Cody

Is any running shoe really worth more than $200? Probably a debate for another day, but unfortunately, this is where the racing shoe market is today. At $280, the Alphafly 4 isn't cheap, and I don't love that price tag. But I also wasn't born yesterday. If you're looking to chase a PR and drop the hammer on race day, this is a shoe worth considering. It's expensive, but I genuinely love running in it, and sometimes that's enough to justify the investment.

Blake

The Alphafly 4 is a great update if you want a more comfortable, accommodating, and stable super shoe without losing that signature Alphafly bounce. It also comes in slightly lighter than the previous version, at 6.7 oz or 190g in a men's size 9, compared to 7 oz or 198g for the Alphafly 3. That said, the difference isn't particularly noticeable on foot.

With Nike technically lowering the price from v3, I think the Alphafly 4 is definitely a solid buy especially with more race day shoes creeping up to the $275-$280 range. However, the Alphafly 3 is still a great shoe and can be found on sale.

Eric

Honestly probably not. The 2 shoes are incredibly similar and with the upcoming Apex being Nikes new pinnacle racer, saving for the new model might be worth it instead.

Buy If

  • You love the signature Alphafly ride and want a softer, bouncier, and more stable racing experience.
  • You’re a midfoot or forefoot striker who enjoys an aggressive, propulsive ride.
  • You struggled with the fit or arch discomfort of previous Alphafly models and want a more accommodating upper.
  • You’re chasing a marathon PR and want a premium carbon-plated racing shoe.
  • You value improved stability, breathability, and comfort over having the absolute lightest race day shoe.

Skip If

  • You already own the Alphafly 3 and are happy with its performance. The updates may not justify upgrading.
  • You prefer a lightweight racing shoe with a smoother, more natural transition.
  • You have narrow feet and struggle with roomier uppers or dialing in lockdown.
  • You dislike the pronounced sensation of Air Zoom pods under your forefoot.
  • You’re shopping on a budget and can’t justify spending $280 on a race day shoe.
